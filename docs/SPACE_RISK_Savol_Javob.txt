MUHIM ESLATMA (taqdimotdan oldin oʻqing)
----------------------------------------
Hozirgi versiya — PROTOTIP (MVP). Sayt hozircha sunʼiy yoʻldoshlardan
maʼlumotni avtomatik yuklab olmaydi. Hududlarning bazaviy xavf indekslari
(0–100) ochiq ilmiy maʼlumotlar va maʼlum faktlar (seysmik zonalar, Orol
inqirozi, iqlim) asosida qoʻlda baholangan. Hakamlar kosmik soha
mutaxassislari — shuning uchun rostini aytish eng kuchli pozitsiya:
"Platforma tayyor, real sunʼiy yoʻldosh maʼlumotlarini ulash — keyingi bosqich."

IMPORTANT NOTE (read before the presentation)
---------------------------------------------
The current version is a PROTOTYPE (MVP). The website does NOT yet download
satellite data automatically. The baseline risk indices (0–100) for each
region were estimated by hand from open scientific knowledge and known facts
(seismic zones, the Aral Sea crisis, climate). The jury are space experts —
being honest is the strongest position: "The platform is ready; connecting
real satellite data is the next stage."

Qisqa javob (taqdimot uchun):
"Biz ESA va NASA'ning ochiq, ilmiy tan olingan missiyalariga tayanamiz —
Sentinel, Landsat, MODIS, GRACE-FO. Hozirgi prototipda bazaviy indekslar
ochiq ilmiy maʼlumotlar asosida baholangan; keyingi bosqichda ularni
Copernicus va NASA API'lari orqali avtomatik hisoblaymiz."

ANSWER (English):
The platform is designed around open, free satellite missions: Sentinel-1
(radar/InSAR: ground deformation, floods), Sentinel-2 (vegetation, surface
water, land cover), Sentinel-5P (NO₂, SO₂, dust), Landsat-9 (land surface
temperature, 40+ year archive), MODIS (temperature, NDVI, aerosols),
GRACE-FO (groundwater), GPM (precipitation) and SMAP (soil moisture). These
sources are internationally trusted — calibrated by ESA and NASA and used in
scientific papers and UN / World Bank reports — so the SOURCES are not fake.

To be honest, though: the current prototype does not yet download this data
automatically. The baseline risk indices were estimated from established
scientific facts (Fergana Valley and Tashkent seismic zones, the Aral Sea
disaster, Surkhandarya heat, the Gazli earthquakes). Forecasts are then built
from these indices, climate trends and AI. So today it is a science-based
estimate, not a direct satellite measurement.

Next step: compute the indices automatically via Copernicus Data Space,
NASA Earthdata, USGS and Google Earth Engine; validate against earthquake
catalogues (USGS/EMSC), Uzhydromet and Ministry of Emergency Situations
data; partner with UzCosmos for national data; back-test on past events
(e.g. the 2020 Sardoba dam failure).

Cheklovlar (rostini aytish kerak):
  - Bu — qaror qabul qilishga yordam beruvchi vosita, rasmiy prognoz emas;
    yakuniy qarorni mutaxassislar va davlat organlari qabul qiladi.
  - AI tavsiyalari umumiy; aniq loyiha uchun muhandislik hisob-kitobi kerak.
  - Uzoq muddatli (25 yil) prognozlarning ishonchliligi pastroq — sayt buni

Qisqa javob (taqdimot uchun):
"SPACE RISK ofatni toʻxtatmaydi, lekin uni oldindan koʻrsatadi va qaysi
chorani birinchi koʻrish kerakligini aytadi. Hokimlik yoki FVV uchun bu —
pulni eng xavfli joyga, eng toʻgʻri vaqtda sarflash degani."

ANSWER (English):
SPACE RISK does not physically "solve" a problem — it cannot stop a drought.
Its job is to show the problem IN ADVANCE and help choose the right decision,
which is the cheapest way to reduce disaster losses (the UN estimates every
dollar spent on preparedness saves several dollars in recovery).

How: (1) it ranks the 9 hazards for each region on a 0–100 scale and puts the
biggest first; (2) it shows how the risk develops over 1, 5, 10 or 25 years,
which helps plan budgets and construction; (3) it gives prioritised measures
— seismic audits of schools and hospitals, mudflow early-warning sensors with
SMS alerts, drip irrigation (up to 30% water saving), saxaul planting on the
dry Aral seabed, green zones and cooling centres against heat; (4) it lists
which satellite data to monitor; (5) the AI analyst answers follow-up
questions such as "What should be done first?".

Limits: it is a decision-support tool, not an official forecast — experts and
authorities make the final decision; AI recommendations are general and real
projects need engineering studies; 25-year forecasts are less certain, which
the site shows as a "Confidence" percentage.

3-SAVOL: Is it connected to real-time satellites?
         (Real vaqtdagi sunʼiy yoʻldoshlarga ulanganmi?)
======================================================================

ANSWER (English):
No — not yet. The current version is not connected to live satellite feeds.
It uses baseline indices estimated from open scientific data, plus climate
trends and AI. The "scanning" animation shown while a forecast is being made
is a visual effect; it does not download satellite images.

Also worth knowing: no satellite system is truly "real-time" for this kind of
analysis. Observation satellites pass over the same place every few days
(Sentinel-2 about every 5 days, Sentinel-1 every 6–12 days, Landsat 8+9 every
8 days; MODIS daily; GRACE-FO gives monthly data). Data becomes available
hours to days later ("near-real-time"). For risk forecasts over 1–25 years,
this is more than enough — what matters is long, reliable time series, not
second-by-second updates.

Plan to connect real data:
  1. Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem / Sentinel Hub API — Sentinel-1, -2, -5P;
  2. NASA Earthdata (AppEEARS, GES DISC) — MODIS, GRACE-FO, GPM, SMAP;
  3. USGS Earth Explorer / Landsat API — Landsat-8/9;
  4. Google Earth Engine — fast processing of all of the above;
  5. A scheduled job (e.g. weekly) recalculates each region's indices and
     the website shows "data updated on <date>".
All of these sources are free for research and public use.

4-SAVOL: If someone asks about our project: what is it for?
         (Loyiha nima uchun kerak?)
======================================================================

ANSWER (English) — 30-second pitch:
"SPACE RISK is an AI platform that predicts future natural and environmental
risks for every region of Uzbekistan — earthquakes, mudflows, drought,
heatwaves, landslides, dust and salt storms, water scarcity, air pollution
and desertification — from 1 to 25 years ahead.

Uzbekistan faces real, growing threats: the Fergana Valley and Tashkent lie
in a strong seismic zone, the Aral Sea disaster sends salty dust over
Karakalpakstan and Khorezm, water is becoming scarcer, and summers are
getting hotter. Today this information is scattered across scientists,
agencies and satellite archives.

SPACE RISK brings it together in one simple website: choose a region and a
time horizon, and in under a minute you get a clear risk score, charts, a 3D
visualisation, prioritised recommendations and an AI analyst you can ask
questions — in Uzbek, Karakalpak, Russian or English.

It is built for UzCosmos, the Ministry of Emergency Situations, regional
administrations (hokimiyats), urban planners, farmers, researchers and
citizens — to spend money and effort where the risk is highest, before a
disaster happens, not after."

Who uses it and why:
  - Government / emergency services — plan preparedness and investment;
  - Hokimiyats and urban planners — where to build, what to reinforce;
  - Farmers and water managers — drought and water-scarcity planning;
  - Scientists and students — research and education;
  - Citizens — understand the risks where they live.

What makes it different:
  - A space-data approach adapted to Uzbekistan's 14 regions;
  - Long-term (up to 25 years) forecasts, not just today's weather;
  - Concrete, prioritised recommendations, not only maps;
  - Works on any phone or computer, in 4 languages including Karakalpak.
